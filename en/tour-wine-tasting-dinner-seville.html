In short

A two-hour wine tasting in a family-owned wine shop near Las Setas in Seville. Six Spanish wines are presented by the shop's own sommelier, who is also the owner: where they come from, which grapes, how they are made, and what to look for in the glass. Food comes with every wine — artisan cheeses, Iberian ham, olives, fresh-baked bread, seafood bites and other regional specialities — generous enough to be a full meal. Private party or small group, maximum 14 people. 87 € per person. This is not my own tour: I book it for you, and I am happy to come along if you would like.

one of my favourites

About the experience

The shop is on a side street near Las Setas, and it looks the way a wine shop should: shelves from floor to ceiling, ladders to reach the top bottles, and a blackboard listing what is open today. This is where people in Seville come to buy wine, and behind the counter stands the owner, a trained sommelier.

We sit down at the long table inside the shop. Six wines arrive in turn — from Rioja and Ribera del Duero, but also from Andalucía and Catalonia — and with each wine comes food chosen because it makes the wine better. The sommelier explains as you go, answers whatever is asked, and lets the conversation run where it wants to.

I do not host this one myself. It is run by the people who own the shop, and that is exactly why it is good: their own cellar, their own suppliers, and a knowledge of Spanish wine I cannot match. I book it for you, and I will gladly join if you want someone to translate and fill in the story around it.

What you take away

01 Six Spanish wines, from bold reds to crisp whites and rosés, each with its own region and its own story.
02 Food with every wine, chosen because it makes the wine better — cheese, Iberian ham, olives, bread and seafood, enough for a full meal.
03 A sommelier who owns the shop himself, explaining grapes, winemaking and how to taste.
04 An introduction to the Spanish wine regions: Rioja, Ribera del Duero, Andalucía and Catalonia.
05 A small, family-owned shop where people in Seville buy their wine — not a place built for tourists.

How the two hours run

Start
We meet at the shop
The shop is near Las Setas, on a side street. You make your own way there, or we walk over together if we have another tour the same day.
Hour 1
The first wines
We sit down at the long table. The sommelier starts light and works darker, explaining what is in the glass and where it comes from, with the food arriving alongside.
Hour 2
Onwards through Spain
The reds and the more complex wines, with ham and cheese to match. By now the conversation runs itself, and questions are welcome throughout.
Finish
Back out into the city
Two hours later you are full, you have tasted six wines and you know something about Spanish wine you did not know before. The shop is central, so the evening can continue wherever you like.
