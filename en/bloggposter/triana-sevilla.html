Triana — the neighbourhood I took too long to discover

By Gunvor · May 30, 2025 · 8 min read
the other side of the bridge

Triana is the neighbourhood on the west bank of the Guadalquivir river in Seville, known for ceramics, flamenco and a strong identity of its own. Triana was long a great unknown to me — the part of Seville I, for far too many years, didn't give the attention it deserves. I regret that now.

On the other side of the bridge

Triana lies on the other side of the Guadalquivir river, connected to the rest of Seville by bridges — and that geographic separation has always given the neighbourhood its own identity. Triana was extramuros — outside the city walls — and that's exactly what defined the kind of people who lived here. Fishermen, potters, sailors, flamenco musicians. And the Roma — the gitanos — who made Triana one of the most important homes of flamenco culture.

The people who live here proudly call themselves trianeros — not sevillanos. The identity belongs to the neighbourhood, not the city. And that says everything about what kind of place Triana is.

«Triana took me far too long to properly discover. But once I began to understand what lay there — the history, the drama, the soul — I regretted every single year I'd overlooked it.»

Carmen — when Triana became world-famous

Even those who've never set foot in Triana know one of the neighbourhood's most famous residents: Carmen. The protagonist of Georges Bizet's 1875 opera is, according to legend, from Triana — a young Roma woman, free-spirited and full of life, who works at the cigar factory and lives life entirely on her own terms. Walking through Triana's streets and hearing flamenco rhythms from a bar, you sense that Carmen isn't entirely made up after all.

Torre de San Jorge — home of the Inquisition for nearly 400 years

Here comes the dark side of Triana's history. Torre de San Jorge, the old Castillo de San Jorge, was the main seat of the Spanish Inquisition in Seville for nearly 400 years. From this tower, thousands of people were interrogated, imprisoned and, in many cases, executed.

Today there's a museum on the site, and it's one of the most harrowing and important places to visit in Seville. Not because it's beautiful — but because it reminds you that this beautiful city also carries a heavy and complicated history.

«Torre de San Jorge was, for almost 400 years, the heart of the Inquisition in Seville. It reminds you that beauty and cruelty can exist side by side — in the same city, the same neighbourhood, the same street.»

The neighbourhood of ceramics

But Triana is also something much more joyful: it was Seville's ceramics heart. This is where the craftsmen worked — the alfareros — who produced the famous azulejos that today adorn walls and floors around the world. The ceramics weren't just used locally, they were exported across the globe — to Latin America, to Europe, to royal palaces and great cathedrals.

Today you can still find craft shops and workshops in Triana keeping the tradition alive — with the same techniques and the same patterns that have defined Triana ceramics for hundreds of years.

Santa Ana — the cathedral that came before the cathedral

Iglesia de Santa Ana is called the Cathedral of Triana, and that's more than just a nickname. Santa Ana is Seville's oldest church, built in 1266 on the orders of King Alfonso X the Wise — long before the cathedral in the centre began to take shape. There's something moving about standing inside Santa Ana and thinking about it: Seville's oldest church sits quietly and slightly overlooked in Triana — just like the neighbourhood itself.

Mercado de Triana — the neighbourhood's new heart

If one place sums up what Triana is today, it's Mercado de Triana — the region's biggest culinary attraction. Inside the market you'll find small food kiosks, traditional stalls with fresh fruit, meat and fish, a cooking school where you can learn to make Andalusian food, and a small theatre for cultural events.

Mercado de Triana isn't just a market. It's a living cultural centre right in the heart of the neighbourhood.

Frequently asked questions

What is the Santa Ana church in Triana?

Iglesia de Santa Ana is Seville's oldest church, built in 1266 on the orders of King Alfonso X the Wise, and is called the "Cathedral of Triana".

What can you experience at Mercado de Triana?

A market with food kiosks and traditional stalls, plus a cooking school and a small theatre for cultural events.

What are trianeros?

Trianeros is the local name for people from the Triana neighbourhood, known for a strong identity of their own on the other side of the river from the centre.
