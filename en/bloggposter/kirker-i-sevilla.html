The 7 most beautiful churches in Seville

By Gunvor · August 12, 2026 · 8 min read
my seven favourites

Seville has some of Spain's most beautiful churches, where history, art and tradition meet. From the mighty Cathedral to the small, hidden Santa María la Blanca — each has its own story. Here are seven I always bring up when guiding.

1. The Cathedral (Catedral de Santa María de la Sede)

In 1401, Seville's leaders decided the city's old mosque should be replaced with a new Christian cathedral — so grand that "those who see it finished will think we were mad." They kept their word. Today it's the world's largest gothic cathedral, a UNESCO World Heritage Site. Beneath the tall vaults lies the tomb of Christopher Columbus, and outside rises the Giralda tower — originally the mosque's minaret, built with a ramp instead of stairs so the muezzin could ride up on horseback.

Legend has it that a sealed chamber inside the Giralda holds the treasures of the Catholic monarchs — but it's never been proven.

2. El Salvador Church (Iglesia Colegial del Divino Salvador)

On the lively Plaza del Salvador stands this baroque church, built over the remains of a Roman basilica and the great mosque of Ibn Adabbas — one of the city's oldest sacred sites. Inside await marble columns and a main altar of almost theatrical detail. One of its most treasured pieces is the Virgin of the Waters, believed to have protected the city from both drought and flood. The church is also the starting point for the Hermandad del Amor's procession during Semana Santa.

«Every church has its own personality — some grand and public, others small and almost secret.»

3. Santa María la Blanca

In the middle of what was once Seville's Jewish quarter, you'll find this small, intimate church — originally a synagogue, converted into a Christian church in 1391. In the 1600s it was transformed into the baroque gem we see today, with carved plasterwork winding across walls and ceiling like lace. Inside hangs Murillo's Last Supper.

Legend tells of a secret passage said to have connected the church to nearby noble houses — used for discreet meetings in turbulent times.

4. San Luis de los Franceses

One of the purest examples of Andalusian baroque. Built by the Jesuits in the early 1700s, with a symmetrical plan where the tall dome draws the eye upward. The interior is a symphony of gold, frescoes and carved woodwork. After years of decline, the church is now beautifully restored, and today also hosts concerts.

5. La Magdalena Church

Formerly part of a Dominican monastery, impossible to miss with its red and ochre façade. Inside, the baroque altars, painted domes and carved figures create an almost theatrical atmosphere. During Semana Santa, the church is the starting point for the Hermandad del Calvario's quiet, moving procession.

Legend has it that the bells once rang by themselves one night to warn of an approaching flood — a mystery still alive in local memory.

6. Basílica de la Macarena

Home to the Virgin of Hope — a centre of devotion unmatched in the city. The basilica was built in the mid-1900s to honour a figure whose significance stretches far beyond Seville. Her glass tears, fine lace and embroidered robes draw thousands of visitors. During Semana Santa, the procession moves through the city at night, surrounded by lit candles and saeta song.

During the Spanish Civil War, the women of the Macarena neighbourhood formed a human chain to protect the figure — a story that still gives the neighbourhood pride.

7. Santa Ana Church — Triana's gothic treasure

On the other side of the river, in Triana, stands Seville's oldest surviving church. Founded in 1276 by King Alfonso X "the Wise," built in gratitude to Saint Anne after the king's recovery from illness. The gothic-Mudéjar style reflects its medieval origins, and among its chapels, Our Lady of the Rosary holds a special place in the hearts of the trianeros.

Legend tells of fishermen who carried small tokens from Santa Ana on their journeys, believing she would protect them from storms at sea.

Frequently asked questions

Which is the most beautiful church in Seville?

The Cathedral is the most famous, but many visitors are most surprised by smaller churches like San Luis de los Franceses and Santa María la Blanca, with their richly decorated interiors.

Is it free to visit the churches in Seville?

Most of the smaller churches have free or symbolic entry, while the Cathedral requires a ticket.

Which church is oldest in Seville?

Santa Ana in Triana, founded in 1276, is Seville's oldest surviving church.
