San Fernando III — Seville's patron saint

By Gunvor · August 16, 2026 · 6 min read
the man behind the city

Every year, on May 30th, Seville celebrates the man who reconquered the city in 1248 — King Fernando III of Castile. He's not just a historical figure, but the city's official patron saint, honoured to this day.

The conquest of Seville in 1248

After a long and demanding siege, Fernando III took Seville on November 23, 1248, ending over 500 years of Muslim rule in the city. It was one of the most important turning points in the Christian reconquest of the Iberian Peninsula (the Reconquista).

«Fernando III is a rare saint one thinks of as a conqueror. But in Seville, the two roles are impossible to separate.»

Rather than destroying Moorish Seville, Fernando and his successors chose to build upon what already existed — clear traces of which we still see today, in the Alcázar and the Giralda tower, both preserved precisely because the Christian rulers recognised the beauty of what they had conquered.

From king to saint

Fernando III was canonised by the Catholic Church in 1671 — over 400 years after his death — and honoured for his piety and his role in reuniting the Christian kingdoms of the peninsula. Today he's the patron saint not just of Seville, but of all Castile.

To Sevillians he's more than a saint from the history books — he's the very founder of the Christian Seville we know today, and his name appears all over the city: streets, schools, one of the city's main avenues bears his name.

«Fernando III is a rare saint one thinks of as a conqueror. But in Seville, the two roles are impossible to separate — he is the city's founder and its protector at once.»

He rests in the Cathedral

Fernando III's relics are kept in a silver shrine in the Cathedral's main chapel, the Capilla Real, which he himself commissioned. The shrine is opened to the public only twice a year — May 30th, his feast day, and November 22nd, the day before the conquest of the city. Visitors can then see his preserved body, dressed in royal robes, a tradition that has continued for centuries.

How Seville celebrates him today

On May 30th, a solemn Mass is held in the Cathedral, often attended by the city's authorities and religious brotherhoods. It's a quieter celebration than Semana Santa or Feria de Abril, but for those who know the history behind it, it's one of the most meaningful moments in Seville's church calendar — a reminder that the city as we see it today was shaped by this one man nearly 800 years ago.

📍 San Fernando III — quick facts
  • Reconquered Seville from Moorish rule on November 23, 1248
  • Canonised as a saint by the Catholic Church in 1671
  • Patron saint of Seville and all of Castile
  • Relics rest in a silver shrine in the Cathedral's Capilla Real
  • The shrine is opened to the public on May 30th and November 22nd each year

Frequently asked questions

Who was Fernando III?

Fernando III was the King of Castile who reconquered Seville from Moorish rule in 1248, and was later canonised as a saint in 1671.

Where can you see the relics of San Fernando?

They rest in a silver shrine in the Capilla Real of the Cathedral, and the shrine is opened to the public only on May 30th and November 22nd each year.

Why is Fernando III important to Seville?

He marked the transition from Moorish to Christian rule in the city, and laid the groundwork for much of what Seville is today, including the preservation of the Alcázar and the Giralda.
