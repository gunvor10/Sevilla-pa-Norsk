What to do in Seville in summer

By Gunvor · August 16, 2026 · 6 min read
hot, but worth it

Seville summers are hot — I won't hide that. But they're far from impossible, and with a little planning, summer can actually be one of the finest times to experience the city. Here's what I do myself.

Contents

See the sights early morning

The Alcázar and Cathedral open early, and that's exactly when you should go. Between 9 and 10am the temperature is still bearable, and the queues are shorter than later in the day. Save the big monuments for the morning, and reserve the afternoon for something calmer.

Find the shade in the middle of the day

Between 2 and 6pm it's too hot for most sightseeing. Do as the Sevillians do: find a shady patio, have a long lunch, or take a siesta. The narrow streets of Santa Cruz and Triana give natural shade, and many bars have toldos — sun awnings that make the terrace bearable even in the middle of the day.

«Summer teaches you to appreciate shade in a whole new way.»

Cool off by the river and swimming spots

A walk along the Guadalquivir always brings a little relief, especially early morning or late evening. If you really want to cool off, it's worth taking a day trip to the beach — the Cádiz coast is under two hours away, and many locals do exactly this on a hot summer day.

Live like a Sevillian in the evening

Summer evenings are when Seville truly comes alive. The streets fill up again from 9pm, restaurants serve dinner well into the night, and the temperature finally becomes pleasant. This is the time for long dinners, nightlife, and rooftop terraces with views over the city.

Indoor options on the hottest days

On the days the temperature really gets extreme, air-conditioned museums and shopping centres are a good option. Museo de Bellas Artes and the many churches (which are often cool even in the heat) offer a break from the sun without having to give up on experiencing the city. Just outside the city there are also water parks like Isla Mágica's Agua Mágica, perfect for a whole day of cooling off.

«The key to a Sevillian summer is shifting your rhythm, not avoiding the city. Do as the locals do — and you'll love it.»

Frequently asked questions

Is it too hot to visit Seville in summer?

It can get very hot, but with the right rhythm — sightseeing early morning, rest during the day, life in the evening — summer is entirely manageable and offers a unique experience of the city.

What do the locals do in summer?

They shift their daily rhythm: avoiding the heat in the middle of the day, and living actively from late afternoon into the night.

Are there nearby beaches from Seville?

Yes, the Cádiz coast is under two hours away by car, and is a popular day-trip destination on the hottest days.
