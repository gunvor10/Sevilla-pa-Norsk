Things to do in Seville — sorted by how many days you have

I have lived in Seville for over thirty years and guided in the city for many of them. These are the places I actually send people to, in the order I would take them — with the time of day to go, and how long you really need.

If you have one day

The five you shouldn't go home without

With only one day, stick to these. They are all within walking distance of each other, and the order below is the one I would follow.

01 Book online

Real Alcázar

The royal palace that is still in use, and to my mind the most beautiful place in the city. Moorish craftsmanship, courtyards with running water, and a garden you can happily lose an hour in.

Best time
Right at opening
Allow
2–3 hours

If you have two days

Across the river and up on the roof

With another day you can fit in Triana, the markets and a proper sunset. This is the day the city starts to feel less like a list of sights and more like somewhere people live.

On the other side of the river, and proud of being something other than the rest of the city. Ceramics, flamenco history, the market, and bars where nobody speaks English.

10 Evening

Flamenco

Seville is the home of flamenco, and there are many ways to see it: a small tablao with or without dinner, free dancing in the arcades of Plaza de España, or a class of your own. Book a few days ahead — the good rooms are small.

Best time
Afternoon or from 9 pm
Allow
About 1 hour

Questions I get often

How many days do you need in Seville?

Two full days covers the main sights at a relaxed pace. With only one day, stick to the Alcázar, the cathedral and Santa Cruz. Three days or more leaves room for Triana, the markets and the park — and for sitting still a while.

Do I need to book tickets in advance?

For the Alcázar and the cathedral, yes. Both sell timed tickets online, and the best slots go days ahead. Book on the monument’s own website, not through resellers.

When is the best time of year to visit?

March to May and September to November. July and August often pass forty degrees in the middle of the day, and the whole day has to be planned around the heat.

Is the city easy to walk?

Yes. Everything in the centre is within half an hour on foot, and the old town is largely free of cars. Bring good shoes — the cobbles are uneven, and it is more walking than you think.

What should I avoid?

Plaza de España in the middle of a summer day, the restaurants with photographs in the menu around the cathedral, and putting too much into one day.
