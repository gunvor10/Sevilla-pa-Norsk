The Gate to America — following the traces of Columbus

For two hundred years the Arenal district was the port of Seville, and all trade with the New World was run from here. We walk through the old shipyard and the riverfront, and finish with a one-hour cruise on the Guadalquivir — the same water the ships sailed out on.

In short

A private four-hour tour of Arenal, Seville's old port district, guided in English or Norwegian by Gunvor Guttormsen, an officially licensed guide living in Seville since 1994. We uncover hidden squares, the medieval shipyard and the buildings that tie the city to the voyages across the Atlantic, and I tell the story of the discovery of the Americas, the trade that followed, and the wealth that poured into the city from the New World. The tour ends with a one-hour cruise on the Guadalquivir, seeing the riverfront, Torre del Oro and the Triana bridge from the water. Guiding costs 245 € for the whole party, with the boat ticket 18 € per person in addition. From 2 to 10 people, your own party only. I collect you at your hotel or we meet somewhere in the centre, as you prefer.

this is where it all began

About the tour

In 1503 Seville was granted a monopoly on all trade with the Americas. No ship could cross the Atlantic without registering here, and no cargo could enter Europe anywhere else. A city sixty kilometres inland became Spain's only port to the New World, and for two hundred years it was the richest in Europe.

All of it happened in Arenal, the quarter between the cathedral and the river. Today people walk through it on their way to the bullring without knowing what stood here: the shipyard where the galleys were built, the customs house, the mint, the warehouses where the silver was weighed. I show you what still stands, and what used to be where the buildings are now.

Columbus himself sailed from Palos, not from Seville — but this is where he came back to, where he raised money and crew, and where he lies buried. His story is more complicated than the one we learned at school, and it is better told in the place where it happened.

How the tour runs

Start
We meet
I collect you at your hotel, or we agree a meeting point in the centre. We walk down towards the river and I explain how the city lay back then: where the wall ran, where the harbour was, where the ships could moor.
Part 1
Arenal and the old shipyard
The quarter between the cathedral and the river. Hidden squares, the medieval shipyard where the galleys were built, the old artillery factory, and the buildings that still carry traces of the transatlantic trade.
Part 2
Torre del Oro and the riverfront
The watchtower from 1220 that controlled the approach, and the quay where the cargo came in. Here I tell you about the monopoly, the ships of the Indies fleet, and what actually arrived in gold and silver.
Part 3
Cruise on the Guadalquivir
An hour on the river that carried the ships out to the Atlantic. Panoramic views of Torre del Oro, the Triana bridge and the quarter where the sailors lived. A calm ending, and the best photographs of the whole tour.
Finish
By the river
We finish where the boat docks, central and close to everything. You get my recommendations for the rest of the day — where to eat nearby, and what is worth seeing next.
